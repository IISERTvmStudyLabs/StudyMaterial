In financial markets, **long** and **short** positions describe the two fundamental ways you can take a stake in an asset:
- 📈 **Long position**: When you **buy** an asset (like shares of a stock or a call option), you are "long." You profit when the price goes **up**.
- 📉 **Short position**: When you **sell** an asset—often borrowing it first, or in our options example, writing/selling a contract to someone else—you are "short." You profit if the price goes **down**, or in the case of selling options, you collect an upfront payment and hope the contract expires worthless.
____
In options trading, **long** means buying a contract (owning the right), while **short** means selling or writing a contract (taking on the obligation). Here are the four foundational positions:
- 📈 **Long Call**: Buying the right to **buy** a stock at a set strike price. You profit if the stock price rises above that strike.
- 📉 **Short Call**: Selling an obligation to **sell** a stock at a set strike price. You profit if the stock stays flat or drops, but face unlimited risk if the stock surges.
- 🛡️ **Long Put**: Buying the right to **sell** a stock at a set strike price. You profit if the stock price falls, acting like insurance.
- 🛒 **Short Put**: Selling an obligation to **buy** a stock at a set strike price. You profit if the stock stays above the strike, effectively agreeing to buy the stock at a discount if it drops.
